Art, Algorithms, and Ownership:
Navigating Artist Rights and AI Data Mining Across EU and US Frameworks

A Comparative Exploration of IP Law, Judicial Precedents, and AI Policy
Ruhi Anand Finn — ADAPT Centre, Trinity College Dublin (rufinn@tcd.ie)

Overview & Motivation

Generative AI presents an existential inflection point for creative labor. As generative models rely heavily on vast datasets of human-created art for training, artists face unprecedented challenges regarding the unauthorized exploitation of their visual styles, moral rights, and economic livelihoods.

This tutorial bridges high-level policy, judicial precedent, and grassroots creative practice—demystifying how algorithms interact with intellectual property law so that creators, legal scholars, and developers can navigate AI governance strategically.

Target Audience

Artists, legal scholars, AI researchers, practitioners, and policy analysts attending JURIX 2026.

Key Outcome

Evaluate human authorship tests and co-design practical TDM policy recommendations.

Tutorial Structure & Agenda

Click each module below to view detailed topics, statutory frameworks, and case precedents:

  • Berne Convention & IP Basics: Overview of copyright protections, moral rights, and economic rights for visual artists.
  • EU Legal Architecture: Key CJEU precedents (Infopaq C-05/08, Levola Hengelo C-310/17, Funke Medien C-469/17, Cofemel C-683/17, Brompton Bicycle C-833/18), Directive (EU) 2019/790 (DSMD), and EU AI Act Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
  • US Legal Approach: Common law copyright, Fair Use doctrine, Transformative Use doctrine, and pivotal litigations (NYT v. Microsoft 2025, Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith 2023).
  • Four-Step Copyrightability Test: Evaluating Domain, Human Authorship Requirement, Originality, and Expression in hybrid outputs.
  • Judicial Review Standards: Applying the Qualified Person Test (Nova Productions v. Mazooma Games, Acohs v. Ucorp) and originality thresholds (Painer C-145/10).
  • Data Ingestion Realities: Model training mechanics, web crawling/scraping, Creative Commons boundaries, and Sui Generis database protections.
  • TDM Battlegrounds: EU TDM exceptions vs. US Fair Use defenses. Case analysis of dataset misuse (e.g., LAION datasets, LLaMA model training).
  • Guided Quad Workshop: Attendees divide into breakout groups (Quads) focusing on four distinct stakeholder perspectives: Artists, AI Developers, Regulators, and Policymakers.
  • Policy Design Focus: Formulating counter-measures including opt-in frameworks, data provenance standards, and equitable remuneration schemes.
