Art, Algorithms, and Ownership:
Navigating Artist Rights and AI Data Mining Across EU and US Frameworks

JURIX 2026 Spotlight Tutorial (90 Minutes)

Presenter: Ruhi Anand Finn | ADAPT Centre, Trinity College Dublin | rufinn@tcd.ie

Overview & Motivation

Generative AI presents an existential inflection point for creative labor. As generative models rely heavily on vast datasets of human-created art for training, artists face unprecedented challenges regarding the unauthorized exploitation of visual styles, moral rights, and economic livelihoods.

This tutorial bridges high-level policy, judicial precedent, and grassroots creative practice, demystifying how algorithms interact with intellectual property law across EU and US jurisdictions.

Key Learning Outcomes

  • Analyse operational differences between EU and US regulatory frameworks.
  • Evaluate judicial tests for original human authorship in AI-assisted works.
  • Co-design scalable policy and technical mechanisms to govern training data collection.

Target Audience

Designed for artists, researchers, legal scholars, practitioners, and policy analysts attending JURIX 2026 seeking to bridge IP doctrine with practical policy design.

Tutorial Program & Schedule

20 mins (15m Pres + 5m Q&A)

Section 1: Foundations of Artists’ IP Rights and Comparative Frameworks

Berne Convention, Moral & Economic rights. Comparative breakdown of EU law (Infopaq, Levola Hengelo, Cofemel, DSMD, EU AI Act) vs. US approach (NYT v. Microsoft, Warhol v. Goldsmith, Fair Use, Transformative Use).

20 mins (15m Pres + 5m Q&A)

Section 2: AI in the Creative Process and Judicial Review

Four-step criteria for copyright protection (Domain, Authorship, Originality, Expression). Application to AI outputs: Qualified Person Test (Nova Productions, Acohs) and Originality (Painer case).

20 mins (15m Pres + 5m Q&A)

Section 3: Training Data, Text & Data Mining (TDM), and Implications

Model training, Creative Commons, web scraping, Sui Generis rights, TDM exceptions, and case studies on misuse (LAION, LLaMA).

30 mins

Section 4: Interactive Policy Lab & Governance Brainstorm

Collaborative workshop in quads tailored for Artists, AI Developers, Regulators, and Policymakers. Focus on opt-in frameworks, data provenance, and equitable remuneration schemes.
